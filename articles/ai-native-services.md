# AI-Native Services: The Fastest-Scaling Business Model in Software History

AI-native services are products and businesses built with artificial intelligence at their core, rather than added onto existing workflows as a feature. AWS's June 2026 "Engines of Growth" report — an independent study of more than 3,400 startup founders and senior leaders across 20 countries — defines AI-native startups as companies under five years old whose products are built around AI. The numbers behind this category are unlike anything the software industry has produced before.

## Growth without precedent

According to the AWS study, AI-native startups now reach billion-dollar valuations in roughly 3.5 years — half the time, and with about half the staff, that it took before generative AI emerged. They report 156% average annual revenue growth, versus 65% for startups overall, and 55% of them generate more than $400,000 in revenue per employee. The Information reported in 2026 that combined annualized revenue at AI-native companies selling models and applications doubled from $15 billion to more than $30 billion in just seven months.

Individual companies illustrate the pace. Cursor, the AI code editor built by Anysphere, went from $100 million in annualized revenue in January 2025 to $1 billion by November 2025 and $2 billion by February 2026 — widely described as the fastest-scaling B2B software business on record, with a reported valuation of $29.3 billion in November 2025. Lovable, a Stockholm-based text-to-app platform founded in 2023, reportedly reached $100 million in annual recurring revenue within eight months of launch and around $400 million by early 2026 with roughly 146 employees. At the model layer, Anthropic reported hitting a $30 billion revenue run rate in early 2026, and its Claude Code agentic coding tool reportedly passed $1 billion in annualized revenue within about six months of its 2025 public launch.

## Demand is broad — results are not

Enterprise appetite explains the surge. McKinsey's State of AI research found that 88% of organizations now use AI in at least one business function, and Gartner predicts that 40% of enterprise applications will feature task-specific AI agents by the end of 2026, up from less than 5% in 2025.

Yet adoption has outrun impact. MIT's 2025 "GenAI Divide" report — based on 52 executive interviews, surveys of 153 leaders, and analysis of 300 public AI deployments — found that 95% of enterprise generative AI pilots delivered no measurable profit-and-loss impact. Notably, the same research found that purchased, deeply integrated solutions succeeded more often than generic internal builds. That gap is precisely the market AI-native services exist to serve.

## Why it matters

The AWS report also notes that AI-native startups cluster in traditional, highly regulated sectors — financial services, healthcare, drug discovery, and cybersecurity — transforming them from the inside rather than at the edges. The lesson of the early agentic era is consistent: AI bolted onto old workflows mostly stalls in pilots, while services designed around AI from day one are compounding revenue at rates software has never seen. The advantage now belongs to businesses that are AI-native by design.
